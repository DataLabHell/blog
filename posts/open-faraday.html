A line-by-line journey through the CO₂ 15 µm band — and the scientific contract an AI agent needs to reason about it safely.

github.com/DataLabHell/open-faraday

The result. At 667.53 cm⁻¹, close to the centre of CO₂'s 15 µm bending band, the CO₂ in a one-metre path of otherwise ordinary air at 420 ppm transmits only 1.13% of an incident monochromatic beam — CO₂ alone, with water vapour and every other absorber excluded. Stretch the same idealized gas to one kilometre and its optical depth is about 4487. open-faraday calculated both through a real MCP session — and returned the assumptions, units, provenance, truncation and validity limits with the numbers.

A metre of air can go dark

Air looks transparent because our eyes sample a friendly part of the spectrum. Move to a wavelength of 14.98 µm and the situation changes. A CO₂ molecule can bend: its two oxygen atoms move while the carbon sits between them, creating a time-varying electric dipole that couples strongly to infrared radiation. Rotation splits that vibration into a dense forest of lines. Near the Q branch, many of those lines crowd together.

Spectroscopists usually label this axis by wavenumber ν̃ = 1/λ, so 667.53 cm⁻¹ is 14.98 µm. CO₂ is a linear molecule: its symmetric stretch does not create a changing dipole in the isolated molecule, while its doubly degenerate bend, ν₂, does. The accompanying rotational transitions form P, Q and R structure. The Q branch is the central pile-up of ΔJ = 0 transitions — not one impossibly strong line, but many quantum transitions overlapping after thermal populations and collisional broadening are accounted for.

We asked open-faraday to isolate the CO₂ contribution between 655 and 680 cm⁻¹ at 296 K and 1.01325 bar, with a mole fraction of 420 × 10⁻⁶. Water vapour and every other absorber were excluded. The 420 ppm value is a round reference and, by 2026, a low one: NOAA reports a 427.55 ppm monthly mean at Mauna Loa for August 2026.[3] This run was not repeated at that value. RADIS performed the line-by-line physics locally using HITRAN data;[1][2] open-faraday supplied the guarded interface around that calculation.

The distinction matters. open-faraday is not a new spectroscopy code or database. Its innovation is the scientific contract: an agent gets a value only together with the context needed to interpret it, and a request outside a defined validity policy is refused instead of being answered with a plausible-looking spectrum.

From molecular lines to missing photons

The molecular-line query found 1361 transitions of the principal CO₂ isotopologue between 660 and 670 cm⁻¹. The tool returned only its first 40 rows, but it did not let 40 masquerade as the whole answer: truncated: true, total_available: 1361, and window_returned_cm-1: 660.007–660.349. Those records cover just 3.4% of the requested window and are the backend's first rows, not its strongest lines. The prescribed next move is to query narrower windows until truncation is false.

This is not bookkeeping trivia. The strongest transition can lie far outside the returned first 3.4% of the window, so taking the largest of those 40 intensities as “the strongest line in the window” is not a noisy estimate; it is an unsupported answer. A Boolean truncation flag says the result is incomplete. The returned sub-window explains how it is incomplete and gives an agent a safe recovery strategy.

The physics in three equations

For a homogeneous absorber, Beer–Lambert relates the transmitted and incident monochromatic intensities:

T(ν̃) = I(ν̃) / I₀(ν̃) = exp[−τ(ν̃)]

Optical depth τ counts e-foldings of attenuation. For fixed pressure, temperature and composition it scales with absorber number density n and path length L:

τ(ν̃) = σ(ν̃) n L,   n = xCO₂ p / (kB Tgas)

RADIS builds the cross-section from the database one transition at a time,

σ(ν̃) = Σi Si(Tgas) φi(ν̃ − ν̃i)

where Si is the temperature-adjusted line intensity and φi is a normalized Voigt profile combining Doppler and pressure broadening. The spectrum covered isotopologues 1–9 and encountered 17 028 lines; with the declared 10⁻²⁷ cm⁻¹/(molecule·cm⁻²) intensity cutoff, 5315 entered the sum and 11 713 did not. That cutoff changes the physics of the calculated spectrum, so it belongs in the result rather than in forgotten documentation.

The ingredients encode different physics. Boltzmann populations and the molecular partition function move intensity among transitions as temperature changes. Thermal molecular speeds give a Gaussian Doppler width; collisions add a Lorentzian pressure width; their convolution is the Voigt profile used here. Close to a dense Q branch, collisional line mixing can also reshape the band, so the sampled maximum is not a universal constant of CO₂. It is a result of a named model under named conditions — exactly why those conditions have to survive the tool call.

What the kilometre means — and what it does not

At the sampled maximum, the one-metre response was T = 0.011254874675236337, hence −ln T = 4.486953939715527. Multiplying by 1000 predicts 4486.953939715527 for a kilometre; the separate tool call returned 4486.953939715522. The 5×10⁻¹² residual is a useful contract check, not independent confirmation of the physics: both values come from the same cross-section, and linear scaling with L is built into this homogeneous model.

Nor does “1.13% transmitted” mean that a warm atmosphere becomes black at 15 µm. Beer–Lambert describes the incident component that survives. A gas in local thermodynamic equilibrium also emits. For a non-scattering, isothermal slab the radiative-transfer solution is

Iout = Iine−τ + Bν(T)(1 − e−τ)

so an optically thick slab forgets the background and approaches its own Planck radiance. In the real atmosphere, temperature, pressure and composition vary with altitude; strongly absorbing parts of the band therefore sample higher layers than the wings. Our kilometre remains a deliberately simple opacity experiment, not a climate model.

A number plus its receipt

The calculation returned 2501 spectral points. To fit the tool transport it sent 498, retaining both endpoints and each bucket's extrema so narrow peaks and dips survive. The response says the array is non-uniform and must not be integrated as though it were the full grid. That is the core design in miniature: limitations travel with the data instead of living on a web page an agent may never read. 

{ "ok": true,
  "contract": "0.3",
  "units": { "wavenumber": "cm-1 (vacuum)",
             "values": "dimensionless (0-1)" },
  "truncated": true,
  "total_available": 2501,
  "provenance": { "source": "HITRAN line-by-line calculation via RADIS 0.17.1",
                  "citation": "…", "accessed_utc": "…" },
  "notes": ["…extrema-preserving decimation…"] }

All five tools — molecular lines, atomic lines, spectra, CODATA constants and the session provenance report — use this versioned envelope. Each tool publishes an output schema, and the test suite validates real responses through an unmodified MCP client. That last phrase is earned: version 0.3.0 published a schema its own serialized nulls violated. Version 0.3.1 fixed the schema and added a protocol-level regression test. A scientifically careful interface must make itself falsifiable too.

ToolScientific role
spectrum_calculateLine-by-line molecular or atomic spectra via RADIS, with the effective conditions and numerical defaults returned.
lines_fetch_molecularHITRAN or HITEMP transitions, isotopologue selection and honest response caps.
lines_fetch_atomicKurucz or bounded NIST ASD windows, including source-specific uncertainty and coverage notes.
constants_getCODATA 2022 values through SciPy, with units, standard uncertainty and exactness.
provenance_reportA paginated, deduplicated bibliography of successful queries in the session.

The metadata is deliberately stricter than the scientific payload. Different databases expose different columns, so data stays flexible; the envelope around it is typed and validated. Units are field-specific: transmittance is “dimensionless (0–1),” while RADIS' quantity named absorbance is “dimensionless (base-e optical depth).” Atomic wavelengths state that they are vacuum values. A client therefore does not have to infer conventions from a plot label or from the name of a Python function.

The provenance is explicit about what it cannot know. This RADIS cache records its download date but not the exact HITRAN edition. The response reports that limitation rather than converting “latest” into a fictional version. Finally, provenance_report() turns the successful calls in the session into a deduplicated bibliography.

MCP is useful here because an agent can discover the tools and their schemas, call the local server over standard input/output and receive structured content. It is still only the transport. The same envelope is returned by an in-process Python call, and the same pattern could be implemented over another protocol. The durable contribution is making scientific context executable at the boundary.

That contribution has neighbours worth naming. Atmospheric science has required machine-readable units for decades: the CF conventions oblige every netCDF variable to carry a UDUNITS-parsable units attribute, and cf-checker reports the files that do not. Astronomy carries the same discipline in query responses, where a VOTable column ships both a VOUnits string and a UCD naming the physical quantity. pint and astropy.units go further still and refuse an incoherent operation at runtime. What none of them checks is whether the question should be put to this data at all. A file checker validates an artefact after it has been produced; a quantity in kelvin is as valid at 1500 K as at 296 K, and a dimension check cannot know that the line list behind it was built for the colder one. That is the check open-faraday moves to the boundary.

Refusal is a scientific result

We repeated the request at 1500 K using HITRAN. Intensities can be rescaled away from their reference temperature, but at high temperature transitions and hot bands that were negligible near room temperature become important. A terrestrial line list can become incomplete while still producing a beautifully smooth curve. 

{ "ok": false,
  "contract": "0.3",
  "error": { "code": "out_of_validity_range",
             "message": "HITRAN is recommended for T < ~700 K, requested Tgas=1500 K." },
  "notes": ["Consider databank='hitemp' … or 'exomol' for higher temperatures."] }

The ~700 K ceiling is open-faraday's conservative policy, not a universal boundary of HITRAN. The scientifically important behavior is that the policy is executable and the refusal names a route forward where one exists — and says plainly when none does, rather than sending the agent to a databank that has no data for the species. A plausible spectrum outside the chosen validity regime would be the more dangerous answer.

open-faraday returns this as a normal structured result with ok: false, not as a broken transport call. That preserves the error code, the physical reason and any suggested alternative for the agent to branch on. “No” is useful scientific output when it says precisely which assumption failed.

From the chat window to the instrument

Our intended next setting is a hyperspectral microscope, where each pixel carries a spectrum rather than three colour values. Calibration checks against known emission lines, feature identification and optical-property retrieval all depend on reference data. If an agent helps plan a scan or flags a feature for another measurement, units, source editions, incomplete windows and validity limits become part of the experimental method.

That is the essence of open-faraday. It does not make a language model into a spectroscopist. It makes the assumptions around scientific reference data explicit enough to inspect, validate and record. Today the question comes from a prompt. Eventually it will come from an instrument. In both cases, the rule is the same: a number travels with its scientific receipt, or it does not travel at all.

Reproduce the experiment
