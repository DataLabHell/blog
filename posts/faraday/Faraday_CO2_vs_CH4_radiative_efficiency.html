Faraday MCP · Greenhouse

Two molecules, one thermal-infrared spectrum

A working demonstration of the Faraday MCP server: six legacy scientific databases, one agent, one end-to-end calculation — why a single molecule of methane traps far more heat than one of carbon dioxide.

A demonstration of the Faraday MCP server — researched & written autonomously by Claude Opus 4.8

Abstract. Authoritative reference data in the physical sciences — fundamental constants, molecular line lists, thermochemistry, reference spectra — remains distributed across heterogeneous, decades-old databases, each with distinct identifiers, units, and access conventions. Combining several of them into a single calculation is consequently laborious and hard to audit after the fact. The Faraday MCP server[1] addresses this by exposing six such databases through one typed, machine-callable interface, allowing an autonomous agent to retrieve and compose their contents within a single chain of reasoning while retaining provenance for every value.

We demonstrate the approach with a line-by-line comparison of the two dominant anthropogenic greenhouse gases, carbon dioxide and methane. Fundamental constants (CODATA[3]), per-transition line parameters (HITRAN[2]), thermochemistry and reference infrared spectra (NIST[4]), and molecular identities (PubChem[6]) are retrieved at run time through Faraday and combined into a radiative-transfer treatment of each gas’s infrared absorption. The analysis resolves a familiar apparent paradox — that methane is by far the stronger absorber per molecule, yet carbon dioxide dominates total forcing — by quantifying the competing contributions of per-line intensity, band saturation, atmospheric column, and Planck weighting. Derived quantities are checked against the published radiative-efficiency expressions of Myhre et al. (1998)[8] and IPCC AR6[9]; every result is traceable to the underlying line parameters, and irrelevant or failed lookups are reported rather than folded into the result.

1The question, and why it needs five sources

The greenhouse strength of a gas is set by how strongly it absorbs the thermal infrared that Earth radiates to space, weighted by how much of that radiation is available at the gas’s absorption wavelengths. To compute it from first principles for CO₂ and CH₄ we need, simultaneously:

h, c, kB, c₂, NACODATAPlanck function, Doppler widths, column densities
M, exact mass, CASPubChemMolecular mass → Doppler broadening; identity/provenance

Two of these rows (WebBook thermochemistry and the IR spectra) are both served by NIST, so the calculation draws on five source endpoints across four distinct providers — out of Faraday’s six upstream archives.

No single database answers the question. The line lists know nothing of the Planck function; CODATA knows nothing of methane’s bending mode; PubChem supplies the molecular mass that sets the Doppler width but none of the spectroscopy. The value of an MCP layer is precisely that one agent can hold all of them in a single chain of reasoning.

The two bands

Both molecules absorb through bending vibrations in Earth’s outgoing longwave spectrum; they are absorption bands, not atmospheric windows. Converting the band positions used here — the CO₂ ν₂ origin and the observed CH₄ ν₄ band maximum at 1305.9 cm⁻¹ (the spectroscopic ν₄ origin proper is 1310.76 cm⁻¹) — with CODATA constants (no hard-coded values) gives:

2Method

Every value below arrives through a named Faraday tool call with explicit arguments: the agent issues the call, the verbatim JSON is recorded, a scipy script computes only from those bytes, and a separate pass re-derives every quoted number.

The numbers in this article are assembled from fourteen Faraday tools spanning the project’s six upstream archives. Fundamental constants — h, c, kB, NA and the first and second radiation constants — are pulled with codata_search and codata_get (CODATA 2022). Molecular identity, molar masses, synonyms and GHS safety for CO₂ and CH₄ come from pubchem_search, pubchem_get_properties, pubchem_get_synonyms and pubchem_get_safety. Thermochemistry (Shomate Cp/H/S) and phase-change points come from nist_search_compound, nist_get_thermo and nist_get_phase_change against the NIST Chemistry WebBook.

The line-by-line absorption that drives the radiative comparison is retrieved with hitran_search_molecule and hitran_get_lines (HITRAN2024); reference spectra are cross-checked through nist_get_spectrum and mona_get_spectrum, and atomic lines through asd_get_lines. Each call — its exact arguments and the verbatim JSON returned — is recorded in Appendix B, and the scipy analysis that consumes those responses is in Appendix C.

3.1 — Per line, methane wins by nearly four orders of magnitude

The HITRAN[2] samples make the first surprise concrete. The strongest sampled CH₄ ν₄ transition has intensity S = 4.78 × 10⁻²⁰ cm·molecule⁻¹; the strongest CO₂ ν₂ transition reaches only 6.95 × 10⁻²⁴. That is a factor of 6,875 between the strongest lines in the retrieved HITRAN windows shown here — a band-integrated comparison would be the rigorous absolute statement, but the per-line gap already shows the direction and rough scale. Methane’s bend is both infrared-brighter and less burdened by the statistical dilution that spreads CO₂’s intensity across a dense thicket of hot-band and isotopologue lines. Zoom in: each stick is one real transition, tooltipped with its lower-state energy and branch.

What this ratio is — and is not

The ~7,000× figure (precisely 6,875×) compares the single strongest line in each retrieved window, line intensity against line intensity. It is not a band-integrated quantity, not a radiative efficiency, and not a GWP: those require every line, spectral overlap, saturation, vertical profiles, and atmospheric lifetime — which is why the established per-molecule radiative-efficiency ratio is ≈33× (section 3.6), not thousands. Read it as a statement about line strengths in these two windows, with the retrieved records to verify it in Appendix D.

3.2 — Line shapes: pressure-broadened Voigt profiles

For this illustrative single-layer calculation, 255 K Planck weighting is paired with 1 atm HITRAN air-broadening parameters; it is not a vertically resolved atmosphere. Using the molecular masses from PubChem and kB from CODATA, the Doppler (Gaussian) half-widths are tiny — 5.75 × 10⁻⁴ cm⁻¹ for CO₂ and 1.86 × 10⁻³ cm⁻¹ for CH₄ — against air-broadened Lorentz half-widths near 0.07 cm⁻¹ from HITRAN’s γair. Lorentz wings therefore set the cross-section. We synthesise σ(ν̃) as a sum of Voigt profiles (scipy.special.wofz):

αD=ν~0c2ln2·kBTmγL=γair·pp0(T0T)nair
Doppler (Gaussian) half-width αD and the air-broadened Lorentz half-width γL (HITRAN parameterisation[2]); molecular mass m from PubChem, kB from CODATA.
σ(ν~)=∑iSi(T)V(ν~−ν~0,i)V=Re[w(z)]αG2π,z=Δν~+iγLαG2
Cross-section as a sum of Voigt profiles V — the Gaussian⊗Lorentzian convolution — evaluated via the Faddeeva function w(z) (scipy.special.wofz)[7], with αG = αD/√(2 ln 2).

3.3 — Curve of growth: how a line saturates as you add gas

A line’s contribution does not grow forever. Its equivalent width W — the integrated fraction of radiation it removes — rises linearly with column density while the line is thin, then bends over into a slow square-root crawl once the core goes black. Tracking the strongest line of each gas and marking the captured scenario columns (NCO₂ = 9.17 × 10²¹, NCH₄ = 4.13 × 10¹⁹ molecule cm⁻²) shows where each sits on its growth curve: the strongest CH₄ line is already into saturation at its core (τ ≈ 9.7), while the strongest sampled CO₂ line is still near the thin, linear regime (τ ≈ 0.32).

NX=xXPsμairg
Vertical column of gas X from its mixing ratio xX and surface pressure Ps (μair = mean molecular mass of air, g = gravitational acceleration).
τ(ν~)=σ(ν~)N,T(ν~)=e−τ(ν~)W=∫(1−e−σ(ν~)N)dν~
Optical depth τ, transmittance 𝒯, and the equivalent width W that traces the curve of growth[10].

The band-vs-line subtlety

Counter-intuitively, individual CO₂ lines are less saturated than methane’s. CO₂’s band saturation is a collective effect: ~15,000 overlapping transitions, acting on a column 222× larger than methane’s (NCO₂/NCH₄ = 9.17 × 10²¹ / 4.13 × 10¹⁹ ≈ 222, a count of molecules — not an opacity ratio), pile up into a quasi-continuum that is effectively black across the Q-branch — which is why the 15 µm band radiates to space from the cold stratosphere. Adding CO₂ can then only widen the band’s edges (logarithmic forcing), whereas methane’s sparser, unsaturated band still absorbs almost linearly with each added molecule.[10]

3.4 — Where the radiation is: Planck weighting

Absorbing strongly only matters where there is radiation to absorb. Earth’s outgoing spectrum peaks (in wavenumber) near 500 cm⁻¹ at 255 K — close to the CO₂ band and far from methane’s. Evaluating the Planck function from CODATA constants, there is 5.0× more spectral radiance at 667 cm⁻¹ than at 1306 cm⁻¹. This is the one factor that favours CO₂: its band is parked on the thermal-emission peak, while methane’s sits on the cooler short-wave flank.

B(ν~,T)=2hc2ν~3ec2ν~/T−1,c2=hckB
Planck spectral radiance per unit wavenumber; c₂ = hc/kB is the second radiation constant (CODATA[3]).

3.5 — Cross-validation against observed spectra

Finally, an independent check that the HITRAN positions are physical: the gas-phase IR absorbance spectra served by NIST’s WebBook[4]. The observed CO₂ band peaks at 667 cm⁻¹ (with its strong ν₃ stretch — literature band origin 2349 cm⁻¹, sampled-grid maximum at 2358 cm⁻¹ in this retrieved spectrum) and methane’s at 1306 cm⁻¹ — exactly where HITRAN places them, from a completely separate measurement and data pipeline.

3.6 — Putting it together: per-molecule potency

The pieces now compose. Methane’s lines are ~7,000× stronger in these retrieved windows and its band is far from saturated, so each added methane molecule absorbs almost in full; CO₂’s band is saturated, so each added CO₂ molecule does very little except at the band edges, even though CO₂ enjoys a 5.0× Planck-placement advantage and a 222× larger column. Net of these competing factors, the established per-molecule (per-ppb) radiative efficiencies are CO₂ ≈ 1.25 × 10⁻⁵ and CH₄ ≈ 4.11 × 10⁻⁴ W m⁻² ppb⁻¹ (IPCC/Myhre[8, 9] simplified expressions, cited literature — not Faraday data), a ratio of ≈33× in methane’s favour. The Faraday-sourced spectroscopy supplies the mechanism behind that famous number; the absolute value is the benchmark it matches.

ΔFCO2=5.35ln(CC0)⟹dFdC=5.35CΔFCH4=0.036(M−M0)⟹dFdM=0.0362M
Simplified radiative-forcing expressions and their per-unit-abundance derivatives (Myhre et al. 1998[8]; IPCC AR6[9]). C in ppm, M in ppb; the CH₄–N₂O overlap term is neglected.

And the resolution of the opening paradox: methane wins per molecule, CO₂ wins per planet — because there is 222× more of it, and because it sits on the peak of Earth’s thermal glow.

4What Faraday is genuinely good for

The honest case for this architecture, on the evidence above. Cross-source chaining is the real win: resolving a species, pulling its line list, scaling it with the right constants, and cross-checking against an observed spectrum happened in one uninterrupted agent workflow, with no manual reformatting between NIST’s, HITRAN’s and PubChem’s conventions. Provenance is intrinsic — because every number arrived through a typed call, the appendices can list each call verbatim, and the calculation reads from the shipped derived Parquet products plus the HITRAN line samples regenerated via those same documented MCP calls (the raw HITRAN/IR tables are not redistributed; see Appendix D). Identifier reconciliation is handled for you: CO₂ is CID 280 to PubChem, C124389 to the WebBook, molecule 2 / isotopologue 7 to HITRAN, and Faraday lets one chain hop between them. For a scientist, the practical effect is that a legacy, human-oriented data landscape becomes machine-actionable in a single reasoning step.

5Limitations

Scientific honesty requires stating exactly what this run could and could not do.

  • HITRAN response limits forced line sampling. The full bands hold 15,327 (CO₂) and 17,771 (CH₄) principal-isotopologue lines; a single call for ~500 lines overflowed the response budget (~300 KB). We therefore retrieved representative P/Q/R windows (90 lines each). Band-centre optical depth and per-molecule efficiency are dominated by the strongest lines, which are captured, so the mechanism and ordering are robust — but the synthesised cross-sections (Figs 2–3) are illustrative, not a full line-by-line opacity, and they under-represent CO₂’s true collective saturation.
  • No absolute forcing is computed from Faraday data. A real W m⁻² figure needs the complete line lists and a vertically-resolved radiative-transfer model with temperature and pressure profiles. The per-ppb efficiencies in §3.6 are the cited IPCC/Myhre benchmark, clearly external; Faraday supplies the spectroscopic mechanism, not the radiative-transfer integration.
  • Cross-sections use HITRAN’s 296 K reference while Planck weighting uses 255 K; full temperature scaling needs partition sums (TIPS) not exposed here.
  • NIST ASD was not used for this molecular problem. The captured run logged earlier ASD challenge/timeout behavior separately; because atomic lines are irrelevant to this calculation, the molecular result does not depend on ASD availability.
  • MoNA is reachable (re-tested 2026-06-01): it returns real experimental mass spectra for valid MassBank accession IDs — e.g. AU100601 yields a 27-peak ESI-MS/MS of sulfaclozine. The earlier "404" reflected an invalid query ID, not an outage. Mass spectra play no role in IR radiative transfer, so MoNA is not used in this analysis.
  • PubChem returned no GHS classification for either gas (both are asphyxiation/flammability hazards in practice); the safety endpoint simply had no structured record to serve.
  • Atmospheric abundances are external scenario inputs (NOAA GML[11]: CO₂ ≈ 427 ppm, CH₄ ≈ 1922 ppb for this captured run), not Faraday data. Refresh those values before using the scenario as a live “today” estimate.

6Outlook

The deeper point is not this one calculation but the pattern it demonstrates. Authoritative reference data lives in fragmented, decades-old, human-facing interfaces — JCAMP-DX files, fixed-width line lists, Shomate tables, constants pages — each a small act of friction. An MCP layer turns that friction into a typed API an agent can compose. A multi-agent pipeline built on these tools is easy to picture: a retrieval agent that resolves species and pulls primary data; an analysis agent that runs the physics under unit and provenance checks; a verification agent that re-derives every number from the raw products and the literature; and a writing agent that produces a transparent, fully traceable artifact like this one. The scientist’s role shifts from plumbing formats to posing the question and auditing the chain — which is exactly the work worth a human’s attention.

Appendix C · Analysis code ▾

The analysis script reads the shipped CODATA/PubChem/thermo/phase Parquet products of Appendix D plus the two HITRAN line samples that you regenerate from the Appendix B hitran_get_lines calls (not redistributed); it uses scipy.constants for physical constants, cross-checked against the Faraday CODATA pull where recorded. build_parquet.py validates the four redistributable products and writes their manifest; running analysis.py before regenerating the HITRAN samples exits with a clear pointer rather than crashing.

"""
analysis.py  (Appendix C)
=========================
Self-contained line-by-line analysis of the CO2 nu2 (15 um) vs CH4 nu4 (7.66 um)
bands.  Reads the shipped CODATA/PubChem derived Parquet products in this
directory PLUS two HITRAN line-sample tables that are NOT redistributed and must
be regenerated first (see the load guard below); uses scipy.constants for every
physical constant (cross-checked against the Faraday-retrieved CODATA values).
Writes results.json consumed by the article.

Dependencies: numpy, scipy, pyarrow  (no network, no hard-coded spectroscopy).
"""
import json, os
import numpy as np
from scipy import constants as sc
from scipy.special import wofz
import pyarrow.parquet as pq

HERE = os.path.dirname(__file__)
PQ = HERE

def load(name):
    return pq.read_table(os.path.join(PQ, name)).to_pydict()

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Constants from scipy.constants  (CODATA), cross-checked vs Faraday CODATA pull
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
h   = sc.h                                              # J s
c   = sc.c                                              # m s^-1
kB  = sc.k                                              # J K^-1
NA  = sc.N_A                                            # mol^-1
c2  = sc.physical_constants["second radiation constant"][0]   # m K  (= h c / kB)
c_cm = c * 100.0                                        # cm s^-1

cod = load("codata_constants.parquet")
faraday_const = dict(zip(cod["name"], cod["value"]))
const_check = {
    "planck_constant":           (h,  faraday_const["planck_constant"]),
    "speed_of_light":            (c,  faraday_const["speed_of_light"]),
    "boltzmann_constant":        (kB, faraday_const["boltzmann_constant"]),
    "avogadro_constant":         (NA, faraday_const["avogadro_constant"]),
    "second_radiation_constant": (c2, faraday_const["second_radiation_constant"]),
}
const_check = {k: {"scipy": float(a), "faraday": float(b),
                   "rel_diff": abs(a - b) / b} for k, (a, b) in const_check.items()}
assert all(v["rel_diff"] < 1e-9 for v in const_check.values()), "CODATA mismatch"
# also verify the identity c2 = h c / kB
c2_identity = h * c / kB

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Molecular / scenario inputs
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
pcp = load("pubchem_CO2_CH4_properties.parquet")
MW = dict(zip(pcp["species"], pcp["MolecularWeight"]))      # g/mol  (PubChem)
m_CO2 = MW["CO2"] * 1e-3 / NA                                # kg
m_CH4 = MW["CH4"] * 1e-3 / NA

# External scenario parameters captured for the article run. NOAA abundances are
# time-dependent, so refresh these before presenting the scenario as "today".
xCO2, xCH4 = 427e-6, 1922e-9
Psurf, g, Mair = 101325.0, 9.80665, 28.96e-3
T_emit, T_surf = 255.0, 288.0
N_air_cm2 = Psurf / ((Mair / NA) * g) / 1e4                  # molec / cm^2 (column)
N_CO2 = xCO2 * N_air_cm2
N_CH4 = xCH4 * N_air_cm2

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Helper physics
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def to_um(nu):       return 1.0e4 / nu                       # cm^-1 -> micron
def to_eV(nu):       return h * c * (nu * 100.0) / sc.e        # cm^-1 -> eV

def doppler_hwhm(nu0, T, m):
    """Gaussian (Doppler) half-width at half-max in cm^-1."""
    return (nu0 / c_cm) * np.sqrt(2.0 * np.log(2.0) * kB * T / m) * c_cm / c  # = nu0/c*sqrt(...)

def voigt_phi(nu, nu0, gammaL, gammaD_hwhm):
    """Area-normalised Voigt profile [1/cm^-1].  gammaD_hwhm = Gaussian HWHM."""
    sigmaG = gammaD_hwhm / np.sqrt(2.0 * np.log(2.0))         # Gaussian std
    z = ((nu - nu0) + 1j * gammaL) / (sigmaG * np.sqrt(2.0))
    return np.real(wofz(z)) / (sigmaG * np.sqrt(2.0 * np.pi))

def cross_section(nu_grid, lines, T, m):
    """Sum Voigt line cross-sections [cm^2/molec]; lines at HITRAN T_ref=296 K, 1 atm air."""
    sigma = np.zeros_like(nu_grid)
    for nu0, S, gair in lines:
        gD = doppler_hwhm(nu0, T, m)
        sigma += S * voigt_phi(nu_grid, nu0, gair, gD)
    return sigma

def planck_wn(nu_cm1, T):
    """Spectral radiance B per unit wavenumber [W m^-2 sr^-1 (cm^-1)^-1]."""
    nu_m = np.asarray(nu_cm1, float) * 100.0
    x = h * c * nu_m / (kB * T)
    return (2.0 * h * c**2 * nu_m**3 / np.expm1(x)) * 100.0

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Load HITRAN samples
#
# These two line-sample tables are NOT redistributed (upstream HITRAN terms).
# Regenerate them via the Faraday calls recorded in Appendix B, then write each
# as Parquet into this directory with these exact filenames and columns:
#   hitran_CO2_lines_651_682cm.parquet  <- hitran_get_lines(CO2, 651..682 cm^-1)
#   hitran_CH4_lines_1289_1322cm.parquet <- hitran_get_lines(CH4, 1289..1322 cm^-1)
#   columns: wavenumber_cm1, intensity_cm_per_molec_cm2, gamma_air_cm1_atm,
#            lower_state_energy_cm1, source_window
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
_HITRAN = ("hitran_CO2_lines_651_682cm.parquet",
           "hitran_CH4_lines_1289_1322cm.parquet")
_missing = [f for f in _HITRAN if not os.path.exists(os.path.join(PQ, f))]
if _missing:
    raise SystemExit(
        "HITRAN line samples not found: " + ", ".join(_missing) + "\n"
        "These are not redistributed -- regenerate via hitran_get_lines "
        "(Appendix B) first, writing the exact filenames/columns documented "
        "above into this directory, then re-run analysis.py."
    )
co2 = load("hitran_CO2_lines_651_682cm.parquet")
ch4 = load("hitran_CH4_lines_1289_1322cm.parquet")

def triples(d):
    return list(zip(d["wavenumber_cm1"], d["intensity_cm_per_molec_cm2"], d["gamma_air_cm1_atm"]))

co2_lines, ch4_lines = triples(co2), triples(ch4)

# band-origin assignments (spectroscopic) + observed cross-check from NIST IR
nu0_CO2, nu0_CH4 = 667.4, 1305.9

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# R1  band identity / conversions
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
R1 = {
    "CO2": {"nu0_cm1": nu0_CO2, "wavelength_um": to_um(nu0_CO2), "energy_meV": to_eV(nu0_CO2) * 1e3,
            "assignment": "nu2 bending fundamental", "iso": "12C16O2"},
    "CH4": {"nu0_cm1": nu0_CH4, "wavelength_um": to_um(nu0_CH4), "energy_meV": to_eV(nu0_CH4) * 1e3,
            "assignment": "nu4 bending fundamental", "iso": "12CH4"},
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# R2  per-line intensity contrast
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
S_co2 = np.array(co2["intensity_cm_per_molec_cm2"])
S_ch4 = np.array(ch4["intensity_cm_per_molec_cm2"])
R2 = {
    "CO2": {"S_max": float(S_co2.max()), "S_median": float(np.median(S_co2)), "n_lines": len(S_co2),
            "band_total_lines": 15327},
    "CH4": {"S_max": float(S_ch4.max()), "S_median": float(np.median(S_ch4)), "n_lines": len(S_ch4),
            "band_total_lines": 17771},
    "Smax_ratio_CH4_over_CO2": float(S_ch4.max() / S_co2.max()),
    "Smedian_ratio_CH4_over_CO2": float(np.median(S_ch4) / np.median(S_co2)),
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# R3  line widths + cross-section spectra over Q-branch windows
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
gD_CO2 = doppler_hwhm(nu0_CO2, T_emit, m_CO2)
gD_CH4 = doppler_hwhm(nu0_CH4, T_emit, m_CH4)
R3 = {
    "CO2": {"doppler_hwhm_cm1_255K": gD_CO2, "lorentz_hwhm_cm1_1atm_typ": float(np.median(co2["gamma_air_cm1_atm"]))},
    "CH4": {"doppler_hwhm_cm1_255K": gD_CH4, "lorentz_hwhm_cm1_1atm_typ": float(np.median(ch4["gamma_air_cm1_atm"]))},
}

def window_sigma(lines, lo, hi, npts=600):
    grid = np.linspace(lo, hi, npts)
    sig = cross_section(grid, [(n, S, g) for (n, S, g) in lines if lo - 5 <= n <= hi + 5], 296.0,
                        m_CO2 if lines is co2_lines else m_CH4)
    return grid, sig

g_co2, s_co2 = window_sigma(co2_lines, 666.0, 668.0)
g_ch4, s_ch4 = window_sigma(ch4_lines, 1304.0, 1308.0)

# optical depth at present columns (from sampled lines -> lower bound)
tau_co2 = s_co2 * N_CO2
tau_ch4 = s_ch4 * N_CH4
R3["CO2"]["sigma_peak_cm2_666_668"] = float(s_co2.max())
R3["CH4"]["sigma_peak_cm2_1304_1308"] = float(s_ch4.max())
R3["CO2"]["tau_peak_present_sampled"] = float(tau_co2.max())
R3["CH4"]["tau_peak_present_sampled"] = float(tau_ch4.max())

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# R4  curve of growth for the strongest sampled line of each gas
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def strongest(lines):
    return max(lines, key=lambda t: t[1])

def curve_of_growth(line, m, Ncols):
    nu0, S, gair = line
    gD = doppler_hwhm(nu0, 296.0, m)
    grid = np.linspace(nu0 - 20.0, nu0 + 20.0, 6000)  # wide enough that W is never window-limited in range
    phi = voigt_phi(grid, nu0, gair, gD)
    W = []
    for N in Ncols:
        W.append(np.trapezoid(1.0 - np.exp(-S * N * phi), grid))   # equivalent width [cm^-1]
    return nu0, S, gair, np.array(W)

Ncols = np.logspace(17, 23, 60)  # present columns (4e19, 9e21) sit mid-range; avoids window-limited tail
co2_strong = strongest(co2_lines)
ch4_strong = strongest(ch4_lines)
nu_c, S_c, g_c, W_co2 = curve_of_growth(co2_strong, m_CO2, Ncols)
nu_m, S_m, g_m, W_ch4 = curve_of_growth(ch4_strong, m_CH4, Ncols)

def tau_core(line, N, m):
    nu0, S, gair = line
    gD = doppler_hwhm(nu0, 296.0, m)
    return S * N * voigt_phi(np.array([nu0]), nu0, gair, gD)[0]

R4 = {
    "CO2_strong_line": {"nu0_cm1": nu_c, "S": S_c, "gamma_air": g_c,
                        "tau_core_present": float(tau_core(co2_strong, N_CO2, m_CO2))},
    "CH4_strong_line": {"nu0_cm1": nu_m, "S": S_m, "gamma_air": g_m,
                        "tau_core_present": float(tau_core(ch4_strong, N_CH4, m_CH4))},
    "N_CO2_present": N_CO2, "N_CH4_present": N_CH4, "N_air_column": N_air_cm2,
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# R5  Planck weighting (why band placement matters)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
nu_planck = np.linspace(400.0, 1600.0, 241)
B255 = planck_wn(nu_planck, T_emit)
B288 = planck_wn(nu_planck, T_surf)
B_CO2_255 = float(planck_wn(nu0_CO2, T_emit))
B_CH4_255 = float(planck_wn(nu0_CH4, T_emit))
nu_peak_255 = float(nu_planck[int(np.argmax(B255))])
R5 = {
    "B_at_CO2band_255K": B_CO2_255, "B_at_CH4band_255K": B_CH4_255,
    "planck_ratio_CO2_over_CH4": B_CO2_255 / B_CH4_255,
    "planck_peak_cm1_255K": nu_peak_255,
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# R6  per-molecule (= per-ppb) radiative efficiency  -- EXTERNAL benchmark
#     IPCC/Myhre et al. (1998) simplified expressions; captured abundances from
#     the NOAA scenario above.
#     CO2:  dF/dC = 5.35 / C           [W m^-2 per ppm]
#     CH4:  dF/dM = 0.036 / (2 sqrt(M)) [W m^-2 per ppb]  (CH4-N2O overlap neglected)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
C_ppm, M_ppb = 427.0, 1922.0
dF_dC_CO2_per_ppb = (5.35 / C_ppm) / 1000.0        # W/m^2 per ppb
dF_dM_CH4_per_ppb = 0.036 / (2.0 * np.sqrt(M_ppb)) # W/m^2 per ppb
R6 = {
    "method": "IPCC/Myhre 1998 simplified expressions (EXTERNAL literature, not Faraday data)",
    "CO2_efficiency_W_m2_per_ppb": dF_dC_CO2_per_ppb,
    "CH4_efficiency_W_m2_per_ppb": float(dF_dM_CH4_per_ppb),
    "ratio_CH4_over_CO2_per_molecule": float(dF_dM_CH4_per_ppb / dF_dC_CO2_per_ppb),
}

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Assemble results + chart arrays
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def stick(d):
    return {"nu": list(d["wavenumber_cm1"]), "S": list(d["intensity_cm_per_molec_cm2"]),
            "Elow": list(d["lower_state_energy_cm1"]), "branch": list(d["source_window"])}

results = {
    "constants_cross_check": const_check,
    "c2_identity_hc_over_k": c2_identity,
    "scenario": {"xCO2_ppm": C_ppm, "xCH4_ppb": M_ppb, "N_air_column_cm2": N_air_cm2,
                 "N_CO2_cm2": N_CO2, "N_CH4_cm2": N_CH4, "T_emit": T_emit, "T_surf": T_surf,
                 "column_ratio_CO2_over_CH4": N_CO2 / N_CH4},
    "R1_identity": R1, "R2_intensity": R2, "R3_widths": R3,
    "R4_curve_of_growth": R4, "R5_planck": R5, "R6_efficiency_benchmark": R6,
    "charts": {
        "stick_CO2": stick(co2), "stick_CH4": stick(ch4),
        "sigma_CO2": {"nu": g_co2.tolist(), "sigma": s_co2.tolist(), "tau": tau_co2.tolist()},
        "sigma_CH4": {"nu": g_ch4.tolist(), "sigma": s_ch4.tolist(), "tau": tau_ch4.tolist()},
        "cog": {"N": Ncols.tolist(), "W_CO2": W_co2.tolist(), "W_CH4": W_ch4.tolist(),
                "N_CO2_present": N_CO2, "N_CH4_present": N_CH4},
        "planck": {"nu": nu_planck.tolist(), "B255": B255.tolist(), "B288": B288.tolist(),
                   "nu0_CO2": nu0_CO2, "nu0_CH4": nu0_CH4},
    },
}

with open(os.path.join(HERE, "results.json"), "w") as f:
    json.dump(results, f, indent=2)

      max(v["rel_diff"] for v in const_check.values()))

"""
build_parquet.py
================
Validate the shipped CO2/CH4 Parquet products and write a manifest.json.

The original generated-source modules used to create these files are not part
of the checked-in blog artifact. This script therefore treats the flat Parquet
files in this directory as the published data layer and records their shape,
columns, byte size, source, and note for reproducibility/audit purposes.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

import json
from pathlib import Path

import pyarrow.parquet as pq

HERE = Path(__file__).parent

# Only the openly redistributable derived tables ship beside the article.
# The raw HITRAN line lists and full NIST WebBook IR spectra are NOT
# redistributed (upstream terms forbid it); regenerate them from the
# hitran_get_lines / nist_get_spectrum calls in Appendix B before re-running
# analysis.py. They are deliberately absent from this manifest.
PRODUCTS = {
    "codata_constants.parquet": (
        "NIST CODATA 2022",
        "Fundamental constants via Faraday codata_get / codata_search",
    ),
    "pubchem_CO2_CH4_properties.parquet": (
        "PubChem",
        "Molecular identity/properties via Faraday PubChem tools "
        "(NCBI places no restrictions on molecular data but cannot grant "
        "third-party rights; CAS Registry Numbers omitted)",
    ),
    "nist_shomate_thermo.parquet": (
        "NIST Chemistry WebBook",
        "Gas-phase Shomate Cp/H/S coefficients via nist_get_thermo",
    ),
    "nist_phase_change_summary.parquet": (
        "NIST Chemistry WebBook",
        "Selected phase-change values via nist_get_phase_change",
    ),
}


def main() -> None:
    manifest = []
    for filename, (source, note) in PRODUCTS.items():
        path = HERE / filename
        if not path.exists():
            # Should not happen for the redistributable set, but skip rather
            # than abort so a partial bundle still yields a manifest.
            print(f"WARNING: missing {filename} -- skipped")
            continue
        table = pq.read_table(path)
        manifest.append({
            "source": source,
            "filename": filename,
            "rows": table.num_rows,
            "columns": table.column_names,
            "bytes": path.stat().st_size,
            "note": note,
        })
        print(
            f"validated {filename:42s} "
            f"rows={table.num_rows:4d} cols={table.num_columns}"
        )

    out = HERE / "manifest.json"
    out.write_text(json.dumps(manifest, indent=2) + "\n")
    print(f"wrote {out.name}: {len(manifest)} Parquet products")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
