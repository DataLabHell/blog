Faraday MCP · Fusion

The Light That Runs the Reactor

A fusion plasma is among the hottest things we build — and untouchable. Almost everything we know about it, and much of how we keep it alive, arrives as light. Here is that light, reconstructed from atomic data pulled through a single open-source MCP server.

A demonstration of the Faraday MCP server — researched & written autonomously by Claude Opus 4.8
Abstract. This article connects a language model to three reference databases — NIST Atomic Spectra Database (ASD), NIST CODATA 2022 fundamental constants, and NIH/PubChem — through the open-source Faraday MCP server, to tell one story: how atomic line radiation both measures a magnetic-confinement fusion plasma and governs whether it survives. Two short case studies follow. (A) The D–T energy ledger, reconstructed from CODATA mass-energy equivalents (Q = 17.589 MeV). (B) The spectroscopy that runs a tokamak — the He II "ash" line as a contactless thermometer, the Dα/Hα isotope split as a fuel gauge, and the tungsten line-forest behind the radiative-collapse risk. The CODATA- and PubChem-backed figures ship downloadable data in two layers (recorded Faraday responses + derived tables); the ASD-backed figure tables ship the derived layer only, since the raw NIST ASD line/level bundles are not redistributed under NIST Standard Reference Data terms (regenerate them via the ASD tool calls in Appendix B). Every number in the prose traces back to a named tool call, and the appendix shows the steps that derive each one, so you can see exactly where each value came from.

1A thermometer you cannot insert

To fuse, deuterium and tritium nuclei must meet at ion temperatures of roughly 10–20 keV — about 100–200 million kelvin, an order of magnitude hotter than the centre of the Sun. Nothing solid survives contact with that, so the plasma cannot be probed by touch. It is read, instead, almost entirely through the light its trace atoms and ions emit: which lines appear, how bright they are, how broad, how shifted.

That same radiation is double-edged. Cultivated deliberately at the cool edge, line emission spreads the exhaust heat into a diffuse glow and saves the wall. Loose in the hot core, it can bleed the plasma's energy away faster than fusion resupplies it and quench the burn. Light is simultaneously the instrument and a control surface.

The atomic data behind every one of these measurements — transition wavelengths, Einstein A coefficients, energy levels, and the fundamental constants that turn them into temperatures and energies — lives in venerable but siloed web portals.[1][2] Faraday's premise is simple: put them behind one machine-callable interface, so a reasoning agent can reach reference-grade values mid-calculation instead of guessing them.

2Method

The loop is deliberately auditable: the agent issues a tool call, the response is written to disk, a scipy script computes only from those recorded responses, and a separate verification pass re-derives each quoted number and shows the steps before publication.

3Case A — from mass to fire

The fuel is heavy hydrogen: deuterium (²H) and tritium (³H). They fuse into helium-4 and a free neutron, and the energy released is simply the mass that goes missing, through E=mc2. Faraday returns the four nuclear mass-energy equivalents directly from CODATA, so the reaction Q-value is a subtraction, not a recollection:

Q=(mD+mT)c2−(m4He+mn)c2=(1875.6129+2808.9211)−(3727.3794+939.5654)=17.589 MeV

The reactants out-weigh the products by 4684.534 − 4666.945 MeV. That difference, 17.589 MeV, is the entire energy of the reaction — and it is just 0.375% of the fuel's rest mass. The whole payoff of fusion is a third of a percent of the mass converted to motion.

That split is the whole engineering problem in one line. The α particle is electrically charged, so the magnetic field holds it: it stays and deposits its 3.56 MeV back into the fuel, and this self-heating is what a burning plasma runs on. The neutron is neutral, ignores the field, escapes, and carries its 14.03 MeV into the surrounding blanket — where it both delivers heat and breeds fresh tritium from lithium (⁶Li + n → T + ⁴He). That is why PubChem's lithium record[3] sits in this story at all.[4][5]

4Case B — the light that runs the reactor

To make α self-heating work you need those ~10 keV ions, and at that temperature the hydrogen fuel is fully stripped — bare nuclei with no electrons, emitting essentially no line radiation of their own. So the plasma is read through the trace species that do radiate: hydrogen recombining at the cool edge, the helium ash from fusion itself, and impurities sputtered from the wall. ASD supplies every one of their lines. Figure 2 places the three key diagnostics on a single axis.

The full per-line table ships as fig2_diagnostic_lines.csv (Appendix D).

The edge gauge — Dα at 656 nm

Balmer-α from recycling hydrogen at the plasma edge is the workhorse monitor of fuelling and recycling. Because a hydrogenic line's wavelength scales with the nuclear reduced mass, deuterium's Dα sits slightly blueward of ordinary hydrogen's Hα. From CODATA's proton- and deuteron-to-electron mass ratios alone:

λDλH=μHμD,μX=meMXme+MXλH=656.2819 nm→λD=656.1034 nm,Δλ=0.1785 nm

That ≈0.18 nm splitting is small but fully resolvable, and it lets the deuterium fuelling signal be separated from any ordinary-hydrogen contamination — an isotope-resolved fuel gauge that needs nothing but a wavelength ratio.

The ash, and the thermometer — He II 468.6 nm

Fusion's product is helium. As α particles thermalise and recombine to He⁺, they light up the He II line conventionally labelled 468.6 nm (ASD Ritz centre 468.5804 nm). Its brightness relative to the Dα line measures the helium "ash" fraction that dilutes the fuel — on real tokamaks the He II 468.6 / H I 656 intensity ratio is exactly how ash is tracked.[6] The very same line is a contactless thermometer: thermal motion Doppler-broadens it, and the broadening encodes the ion temperature directly.

Δλλ=8ln2·kTmc2(kT and mc2 in the same energy unit)

Keeping kT and mc2 in identical energy units means no constant ever slips into the square root with the wrong unit system — the single most common bug in this calculation. A He⁺ ion at 10 keV broadens the 468.5804 nm line to a full-width of 1.807 nm: a hundred million kelvin written straight into a linewidth. The width grows as √T — 0.571 nm at 1 keV, 1.807 nm at 10 keV, 2.556 nm at 20 keV — which is why a single calibrated spectrometer reads the core temperature.

Radiation that saves, radiation that kills

Line radiation is not only a diagnostic — it is a power-handling tool, and a hazard. The exhaust heat that would otherwise melt the divertor can be radiated away as a diffuse glow by deliberately seeding light impurities such as nitrogen or neon into the edge, a regime called detachment.[8][9] The same physics turns lethal with heavy elements. Tungsten — the plasma-facing armour, PubChem record tungsten (W, 183.84 g/mol, CID 23964) — is never fully stripped even in the core, so it keeps bound electrons that radiate prodigiously across a forest of lines.

A clue hiding in the data
In a single matched 10 nm window (400–410 nm), neutral tungsten saturated ASD's 200-line return cap — the response literally overflowed the MCP transport — while the low-Z seeding ion N II returned just 5 lines. That ≥40× contrast in line density is the visible signature of why a trace of tungsten in the core can radiate the plasma's energy away faster than fusion replaces it: a radiative collapse. Low-Z at the edge, good; high-Z in the core, fatal.[9]

5Limitations

The honest boundary of this exercise matters more than its figures. Four limitations:

A real transport ceiling
Wide ASD windows overflow the MCP response limit into an out-of-band file the sandbox cannot read. We tile narrow windows instead — and recorded the overflow itself as a datum, because it is exactly what exposed tungsten's line density. The query strategy lives in the line-density log bundle (asd_line_density_log.json), regenerated via the ASD calls in Appendix B rather than shipped, since NIST ASD line data is not redistributed here.
The line-density contrast is an illustration, not a cooling rate
N II is itself line-rich over wider bands; the clean ≥40× ratio holds only in the matched 400–410 nm window. The actual high-Z danger is quantified by collisional-radiative cooling factors (e.g. ADAS), which Faraday does not serve. We state this rather than working around it.[9]
The thermometer is idealised
Figure 3 is pure thermal Doppler broadening of a single He⁺ population. Real ion-temperature spectroscopy uses active charge-exchange (CXRS/CER) and must subtract Zeeman, Stark, fine-structure and instrumental contributions.[6][7] Our number is the textbook thermal width — correct as such, but a floor on real linewidths.
Numerical note — the α/neutron split
A drafting agent would likely have asserted "3.5 / 14.1 MeV" from memory. Computed instead: cold-reactant relativistic two-body kinematics gives 3.56 / 14.03 MeV. The familiar 3.5 / 14.1 figures round these and fold in the reactants' thermal energy; the exact, robust statement is the 1/5 : 4/5 share, which is what we quote. This is the discipline working as intended — the number was derived, then reconciled with the textbook, not copied from it.

6Outlook

The interesting result here is not any single figure; it is that an agent can now assemble a genuinely cross-database calculation — ASD lines, CODATA constants, and PubChem materials in one reasoning pass — while preserving the raw bytes for audit. Fold in HITRAN, already part of Faraday, and the same loop extends to line-by-line radiative transfer for the cold divertor plasma; add collisional-radiative data and it reaches true spectral synthesis. The unit of progress is the verifiable pipeline, not the headline number. Reference data has spent decades behind portals built for humans clicking; the work here is a small proof that it can also be reached, correctly and transparently, by the models now doing the reasoning.

Appendix C · Analysis code ▾

The analysis is a single standalone script. load_from_raw() reads the raw JSON bundles named below and returns the exact inputs the derivations expect, so running analysis.py regenerates results.json — the Q-value, the Doppler widths, the isotope shift. The CODATA and PubChem bundles ship with the repo; the NIST ASD line bundles (asd_*.json) are not redistributed under NIST Standard Reference Data terms (see data/DATA_NOTICE.md) — regenerate them first via the Faraday ASD calls in Appendix B, after which the script runs with no further tool calls (when they are absent load_from_raw() skips them with a warning and only the CODATA D–T ledger is recomputed). Save each ASD response under the exact filename load_from_raw() reads — asd_he_ii_400_700.json, asd_h_i_655_657.json, asd_w_i_350_550.json, asd_n_ii_400_410.json (each a {"element": …, "lines": […]} object with the ASD line fields) — plus asd_line_density_log.json carrying a matched_window_contrast object with keys window_nm, W_I_lines, N_II_lines, ratio_floor. An independent cross-check compares Faraday's CODATA values with scipy.constants (CODATA 2022) and reports the worst floating relative difference in the script output.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Faraday MCP -> fusion plasma spectroscopy: analysis.

Every physical input is loaded from the published raw-response bundles in this
directory (load_from_raw()) -- the constants and atomic lines that the article
quotes are the same bytes Faraday returned. No value is hardcoded from memory.

Data availability: the CODATA and PubChem raw bundles ship with the repo, so the
D-T energy ledger runs out of the box. The NIST ASD line bundles
(asd_*.json, asd_line_density_log.json) are NOT redistributed under NIST Standard
Reference Data terms; regenerate them first via the ASD MCP calls in Appendix B.
When they are absent load_from_raw() skips them with a warning and the ASD-backed
derivations (Doppler, isotope shift, line density, diagnostic map) are skipped.

Three derivations:
  (A) The D-T reaction energy ledger      -> CODATA mass-energy equivalents
  (B) Doppler thermometry of He II 468.6  -> CODATA k_B/c/masses + ASD line
  (C) The H/D isotope shift of Balmer-a   -> CODATA mass ratios + ASD line

Unit-safety note (cf. the canonical Doppler sqrt bug): the Doppler width is
evaluated with kT and mc^2 in the SAME energy unit (keV), so no SI/CGS constant
ever enters the square root. scipy.constants (CODATA 2022) is used ONLY as an
independent auditor of the Faraday values, never as the source.
"""
import json, math, warnings
from pathlib import Path

RAW = Path(__file__).parent

ASD_NOTE = ("regenerate the ASD bundles via the ASD MCP calls in Appendix B "
            "(not redistributed under NIST Standard Reference Data terms)")

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# load_from_raw(): reconstruct every analysis input from the shipped raw JSON.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def load_from_raw(raw_dir: Path = RAW) -> dict:
    def rd(name):
        return json.loads((raw_dir / name).read_text())

    def rd_asd(name):
        # ASD bundles are not redistributed; skip-with-warning when absent.
        try:
            return rd(name)
        except FileNotFoundError:
            warnings.warn(f"missing ASD bundle {name!r}; {ASD_NOTE}")
            return None

    codata_list = rd("codata_constants.json")["records"]
    codata = {r["name"]: r for r in codata_list}

    he2 = rd_asd("asd_he_ii_400_700.json")
    h1  = rd_asd("asd_h_i_655_657.json")
    w1  = rd_asd("asd_w_i_350_550.json")
    n2  = rd_asd("asd_n_ii_400_410.json")
    dens = rd_asd("asd_line_density_log.json")
    pub = rd("pubchem_materials.json")
    return dict(codata=codata, he2=he2, h1=h1, w1=w1, n2=n2, dens=dens, pub=pub)


def C(codata, name):
    """CODATA value as float (handles '(exact)' uncertainties)."""
    return float(codata[name]["value"])


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Derivation A -- the D-T energy ledger
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def dt_energy(codata):
    mD = C(codata, "deuteron mass energy equivalent in MeV")
    mT = C(codata, "triton mass energy equivalent in MeV")
    mA = C(codata, "alpha particle mass energy equivalent in MeV")
    mN = C(codata, "neutron mass energy equivalent in MeV")

    Q = (mD + mT) - (mA + mN)                       # MeV released

    # Relativistic two-body partition, cold reactants (p_alpha = -p_n).
    # Total invariant energy W = mD + mT (reactants ~at rest).
    W = mD + mT
    # E_alpha_total = (W^2 + mA^2 - mN^2) / (2W);  KE = E_total - rest energy
    E_alpha_tot = (W*W + mA*mA - mN*mN) / (2.0 * W)
    KE_alpha = E_alpha_tot - mA
    KE_n = Q - KE_alpha

    # mass defect as a fraction of reactant rest energy
    frac = Q / (mD + mT)
    return dict(mD=mD, mT=mT, mA=mA, mN=mN, Q_MeV=Q,
                KE_alpha_MeV=KE_alpha, KE_n_MeV=KE_n,
                alpha_share=KE_alpha/Q, n_share=KE_n/Q,
                mass_defect_frac=frac)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Derivation B -- Doppler thermometry of the He II 468.6 nm ash line
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def doppler(codata, he2):
    # Strongest He II component in the 468.5 nm n=4->3 cluster (largest Aki).
    lines = he2["lines"]
    cluster = [l for l in lines if l["ritz_wl_air_nm"].startswith("468")]
    strongest = max(cluster, key=lambda l: float(l["Aki_s1"]))
    lam0_nm = float(strongest["ritz_wl_air_nm"])

    # He+ rest energy = alpha + one bound electron (the emitter is He II = He+).
    mc2_MeV = (C(codata, "alpha particle mass energy equivalent in MeV")
               + C(codata, "electron mass energy equivalent in MeV"))
    mc2_keV = mc2_MeV * 1.0e3

    EIGHT_LN2 = 8.0 * math.log(2.0)

    def fwhm_nm(T_keV):
        # Delta-lambda_FWHM / lambda = sqrt(8 ln2 * kT / mc^2); kT,mc^2 in keV.
        return lam0_nm * math.sqrt(EIGHT_LN2 * T_keV / mc2_keV)

    grid = [1, 2, 4, 6, 10, 15, 20]
    table = [dict(T_keV=T, fwhm_nm=fwhm_nm(T),
                  sigma_nm=fwhm_nm(T)/(2*math.sqrt(2*math.log(2)))) for T in grid]

    # Gaussian profiles for the figure (normalised peak = 1).
    profiles = {}
    half = 3.0  # +/- nm window
    npts = 241
    xs = [lam0_nm - half + i*(2*half)/(npts-1) for i in range(npts)]
    for T in [1, 4, 10, 20]:
        sigma = fwhm_nm(T)/(2*math.sqrt(2*math.log(2)))
        profiles[T] = [math.exp(-((x-lam0_nm)**2)/(2*sigma*sigma)) for x in xs]

    return dict(lam0_nm=lam0_nm, emitter="He II (He+)",
                mc2_MeV=mc2_MeV, fwhm_table=table,
                fwhm_10keV_nm=fwhm_nm(10.0),
                profile_x_nm=xs, profiles=profiles,
                strongest_Aki_s1=float(strongest["Aki_s1"]))


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Derivation C -- H/D isotope shift of Balmer-alpha
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def isotope_shift(codata, h1):
    # Composite Balmer-alpha wavelength (what a spectrometer resolves).
    comp = [l for l in h1["lines"] if l.get("ritz_wl_air_nm", "").startswith("656.28")]
    lam_H = float(comp[0]["ritz_wl_air_nm"]) if comp else 656.2819

    rp = C(codata, "proton-electron mass ratio")     # M_p / m_e
    rd = C(codata, "deuteron-electron mass ratio")    # M_d / m_e

    # reduced-mass factors in units of m_e: mu/m_e = 1/(1 + m_e/M)
    muH = 1.0 / (1.0 + 1.0/rp)
    muD = 1.0 / (1.0 + 1.0/rd)
    ratio = muH / muD                                 # lambda_D / lambda_H
    lam_D = lam_H * ratio
    dlam = lam_H - lam_D                               # D is bluer
    return dict(lam_H_nm=lam_H, lam_D_nm=lam_D, dlam_nm=dlam,
                muH_over_me=muH, muD_over_me=muD)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Line-density contrast (high-Z vs low-Z), from the density log
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def line_density(dens):
    m = dens["matched_window_contrast"]
    return dict(window_nm=m["window_nm"], W_I=m["W_I_lines"],
                N_II=m["N_II_lines"], ratio_floor=m["ratio_floor"])


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Diagnostic-line table for the spectral-map figure
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
def diagnostic_lines(data):
    rows = []
    # D-alpha / H-alpha (use composite + computed D partner)
    iso = isotope_shift(data["codata"], data["h1"])
    rows.append(dict(species="H I (H-alpha)", wavelength_nm=iso["lam_H_nm"],
                     Aki_s1=4.4101e7, role="fuel recycling / edge (H)"))
    rows.append(dict(species="D I (D-alpha, computed)", wavelength_nm=iso["lam_D_nm"],
                     Aki_s1=4.4101e7, role="fuel recycling / edge (D)"))
    # He II 468.6 strongest components
    for l in data["he2"]["lines"]:
        if l["ritz_wl_air_nm"].startswith("468") and float(l["Aki_s1"]) > 4e7:
            rows.append(dict(species="He II (ash)", wavelength_nm=float(l["ritz_wl_air_nm"]),
                             Aki_s1=float(l["Aki_s1"]), role="fusion ash / proof-of-burn"))
    # W I forest (sample)
    for l in data["w1"]["lines"]:
        wl = l.get("obs_wl_air_nm") or l.get("ritz_wl_air_nm")
        try:
            intens = float(str(l.get("intens","")).rstrip("*w"))
        except ValueError:
            intens = float("nan")
        rows.append(dict(species="W I (wall)", wavelength_nm=float(wl),
                         Aki_s1=float("nan"), role="tungsten influx / impurity",
                         intens=intens))
    return rows


def crosscheck_scipy(codata):
    """Independent auditor: Faraday CODATA values vs scipy.constants (CODATA 2022)."""
    try:
        from scipy import constants as sc
    except Exception as e:
        return {"available": False, "error": str(e)}
    pc = sc.physical_constants
    checks = {}
    pairs = [
        ("deuteron mass energy equivalent in MeV", "deuteron mass energy equivalent in MeV"),
        ("triton mass energy equivalent in MeV",   "triton mass energy equivalent in MeV"),
        ("alpha particle mass energy equivalent in MeV", "alpha particle mass energy equivalent in MeV"),
        ("neutron mass energy equivalent in MeV",  "neutron mass energy equivalent in MeV"),
        ("Boltzmann constant",                     "Boltzmann constant"),
        ("speed of light in vacuum",               "speed of light in vacuum"),
        ("proton-electron mass ratio",             "proton-electron mass ratio"),
        ("deuteron-electron mass ratio",           "deuteron-electron mass ratio"),
    ]
    worst = 0.0
    for far_name, sci_name in pairs:
        fv = C(codata, far_name)
        sv = sc.value(sci_name) if sci_name in pc else getattr(sc, {
            "Boltzmann constant": "k", "speed of light in vacuum": "c"}.get(sci_name, ""), None)
        sv = float(sv)
        rel = abs(fv - sv) / abs(sv) if sv else float("nan")
        worst = max(worst, rel)
        checks[far_name] = dict(faraday=fv, scipy=sv, rel_diff=rel)
    return {"available": True, "worst_rel_diff": worst, "checks": checks,
            "scipy_version": sc.__version__ if hasattr(sc, "__version__") else "n/a"}


def main():
    data = load_from_raw()
    cod = data["codata"]

    # CODATA-only derivation always runs.
    A = dt_energy(cod)
    xchk = crosscheck_scipy(cod)

    # ASD-backed derivations run only when the bundles were regenerated.
    have_asd = all(data[k] is not None for k in ("he2", "h1", "w1", "n2", "dens"))
    B = doppler(cod, data["he2"]) if data["he2"] else None
    Cc = isotope_shift(cod, data["h1"]) if data["h1"] else None

def write_csv(path, header, rows):
    with open(path, "w", newline="") as f:
        w = csv.writer(f)
        w.writerow(header)
        w.writerows(rows)


def write_parquet(path, header, rows):
    cols = list(zip(*rows)) if rows else [[] for _ in header]
    arrays = []
    for name, col in zip(header, cols):
        # infer: all-numeric -> double, else string
        try:
            arrays.append(pa.array([None if v == "" else float(v) for v in col], type=pa.float64()))
        except (ValueError, TypeError):
            arrays.append(pa.array([str(v) for v in col], type=pa.string()))
    pq.write_table(pa.table(arrays, names=header), path)


def product(name, folder, header, rows):
    write_csv(folder / f"{name}.csv", header, rows)
    write_parquet(folder / f"{name}.parquet", header, rows)
    return dict(name=name, rows=len(rows), cols=header)
